Title
When a Language Model Server Reports a Different Token

Authors
Yufeiyang Chen

Abstract
Applications route queries, grade responses, calibrate uncertainty, and update reinforcement-learning policies using the log probability attached to each generated token. Does the reported probability actually belong to the token that was sampled? Toggling candidate-list inclusion (top_logprobs) on the identical sampling event exposes a deterministic attribution substitution in eight consecutive releases of MLX-LM (0.30.6-0.32.2): requesting alternatives overwrites the outer selected entry with the first candidate while emitting intact generated text. Formalizing five contract invariants (I1-I5) across seven serving architectures separates ID/score substitution and streaming omission in MLX-LM (528/1,488 IFEval positions; confirmed on Qwen2.5-3B and Qwen2.5-7B) from empty-delta streaming drops in SGLang v0.5.23 (78/246 positions cured by toggling reasoning_parser or a one-condition fix), deferred UTF-8 fragment representations in vLLM v0.34.0 (102/246), EOS/UTF-8 coalescing row shortfalls and top_logprobs=5 stochastic drift in live Ollama v0.40.17, row omission in llama.cpp (3/280), UTF-8 byte corruption in mistral.rs (708 FLORES-200 positions), and cross-row batch callback leakage in CTranslate2 4.9.0 (24/24 equal-length steps). Our 15-check full-transport patch passes all 384 live positions across SmolLM2-135M, Qwen2.5-3B, and Qwen2.5-7B with streaming parity. Downstream across both 3B and 7B Qwen2.5 checkpoints on ARC-Challenge (N=250 per model), wire inflation (+0.194 and +0.118 nats) induces a calibration paradox--lowering aggregate ECE (0.125 to 0.032 at 3B; 0.100 to 0.035 at 7B) while raising wrong-answer false acceptance by +49.2 pp at 3B (p < 1e-6) and +33.3 pp to +35.7 pp at 7B (p <= 1.2e-4). In LLM-as-a-Judge evaluation (DeepEval 4.3.4), broken span matching flips 6/94 pass verdicts at 3B before attenuating to 2/95 score shifts (0 flips) at 7B, whereas in 200-rollout Qwen2.5-7B GRPO trajectories, strictly over-reported wire scores (\hat{s}_t > s_t) cause asymmetric clipped-PPO distortion: zeroing clipped-PPO surrogate gradients on 86.4% (95/110) of corrupted negative-advantage tokens (epsilon = 0.2), attenuating the surrogate gradient on 100% (92/92) of corrupted positive-advantage tokens, and injecting nonzero spurious Step-0 KL divergence in 181/200 rollouts, alongside a -1.90 pp held-out accuracy drop across 128 online RL seeds.
